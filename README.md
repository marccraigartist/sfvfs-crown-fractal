# SFVFS Crown Fractal

[Open the interactive website](https://marccraigartist.github.io/sfvfs-crown-fractal/)

A two-dimensional visual study of Marc Craig's SFVFS three-axis gyroscope: A/X opposed crowns, recursive rings, a central hexagon and star projection, moving crowns, and rainbow fans. All three axes begin at B12 and share one twelve-seat clock.

Use the controls to pause, restart at B12, change speed, scrub one cycle, turn the six geometry layers on or off, and zoom into the recursive children. Drag or pinch to explore.

The rainbow fans use eight known zeta-zero heights. Their crown angles can be enlarged up to 20× for display. This is a visualization, not a prime or zero predictor or a proof of the Riemann hypothesis.

The complete program is in [index.html](index.html); it has no external runtime dependencies.

## Shared-seat holonomy explorer

[Open the loop and address explorer](https://marccraigartist.github.io/sfvfs-crown-fractal/holonomy.html) · [Read the mathematical notes](HOLONOMY_NOTES.md)

This companion examines the 30 distinct stations formed by 36 ring slots, including six shared hubs. Compare nine-edge octant loops and twelve-edge ring or mixed loops, then run 144 or 1,728 graph moves. Closed routes have measured spherical parallel-transport holonomy. A separate lab compares order-sensitive similarity-map addresses and an exact projected overlap of two distinct 3D points.

The shared hubs make graph routes meet; they do not imply different finite addresses over the 30 unique maps land on the same 3D point. These geometries are established constructions used as an SFVFS visual study, with no claim of a prime predictor or RH proof.
